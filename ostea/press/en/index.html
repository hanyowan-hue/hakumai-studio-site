About Ostea

A 3D puzzle where you rebuild the skeletons of dinosaurs and other animals one bone at a time, then work out from the shape of the bones how each animal lived.

A giraffe has 7 neck bones, the same number as a human, yet each one can be more than 25 cm long. Tyrannosaurus teeth were not thin like knives but thick like bananas. What were those thick teeth for?

The bones start out scattered. You drag each one to where it belongs on the body. When the last bone fits, the skeleton stands in a museum hall and the animal's plate is drawn into an illustrated encyclopedia.

Most of the skeletons are made from 3D scans of specimens held by universities and museums, including the Natural History Museum of the University of Pisa. There are 36 skeletons. The 5 in the first volume (Triceratops, Cat, Human, Giraffe and Bottlenose dolphin) are free, and there are no ads.

Features

A Bone quiz about how animals lived

Three-choice questions you answer while looking at the skeleton you just built: 49 questions across 34 animals. Every answer comes with an explanation and a cited source.

  • What did these thick teeth do?Tyrannosaurus
  • What is this long neck for?Giraffe
  • What is this long tusk mainly for?Narwhal

Skeletons made from scans of real specimens

Most of the skeletons come from 3D scans of skeleton specimens in university and museum collections. Others come from scans of display replicas, from medical CT data, or from reconstructed models. Each specimen states how it was made and lists its sources.

Playable by ear with VoiceOver

With VoiceOver on, the puzzle becomes bone identification: you hear a clue written for each bone and choose where on the body it belongs. The encyclopedia and the Bone quiz can be read with VoiceOver too, in all 10 languages.

  1. Clue"It has three horns and a wide, spreading bony frill."
  2. Wrong place"Closer to the tail"
  3. Right placeThe bone snaps in with a sound and a haptic

Take out a single bone to study it

Tap a bone on a finished skeleton to see its name. You can take out a single bone and read its Latin name and a note on its features.

A museum hall or a habitat scene

Turn a finished skeleton to study it from any angle, in the museum hall or in a scene of the animal's habitat, such as a forest or underwater. On supported devices, AR places it in your room at life size.

36 skeletons, from dinosaurs to sea animals

Five volumes: First specimens (5, free), Dinosaurs and ancient creatures (9), Land animals (9), Sea animals (6), and Large animals of forests and grasslands (7). Each skeleton you complete adds a plate to the encyclopedia.

Choose your difficulty

Play in Easy or Normal mode, and the Human also has Expert. Available levels vary by specimen. Turn on the timer only when you want it.

One-time purchases, no ads

The first volume (5 skeletons) is free. The other 4 volumes are one-time purchases, sold individually or as a bundle. There are no ads and no subscriptions.

From the developer

I have always loved dinosaurs, and what fascinated me was that the features of a bone show how the animal lived. While making Ostea, I kept being surprised. The hip of Tyrannosaurus, for example: the pubis reaches forward and ends in a broad foot like the sole of a boot, nothing like any animal alive today. The narwhal's long horn turned out to be a tooth. And a shark like megalodon has not a single piece of hard bone. I would be happiest if children enjoyed playing and it got them thinking about things like this.

Developer, Hakumai Studio

How the skeletons and art were made

Each specimen in the app states whether its 3D model comes from a scan of a real specimen, a scan of a display replica, medical CT data or a reconstructed model, and lists its sources. Many of the scans were shared under Creative Commons licences by the people who scanned the specimens.

The encyclopedia illustrations and background art were made with AI image generation. Some launch screens use 19th-century natural history plates in the public domain.
